# Camillo speculative underwriting mode

Revision: September 26, 2026 — market-recognition / expectations-gap underwriting. Read ../../investment-strategy-lanes/SKILL.md. This is the Camillo mode of Full Underwriting, not the Event Reaction mechanical strategy and not a looser version of Core valuation.

## Decision question

**Has something economically meaningful changed, does the market appear to under-recognize the consequence, and does this exact security/instrument still offer enough remaining recognition payoff from today's price to justify speculative allocation review?**

Retain the RWC starting evidence and case identity. Recheck current decision-sensitive facts. Distinguish fact, inference and the bet. Keep the opening answer simple and preserve an actual speculative judgment.

## Core principle: expectations gap, not just valuation gap

Camillo underwriting has a different center of gravity from Core:

- **Core** asks whether the security is attractive relative to durable business value and required return.
- **Camillo** asks whether a meaningful public-information change is likely to alter market understanding before that consequence is fully reflected.

A security can fail Core's valuation test and still survive Camillo underwriting when a credible under-recognized change leaves asymmetric remaining payoff. A security can also look cheap under Core while offering no Camillo edge because the relevant change is already broadly understood.

Do **not** state `priced in` or `not priced in` as an observable fact. Market expectations are inferred. Use dated evidence and an explicit recognition state:

- **LOW** — little direct evidence that the specific consequence is in mainstream investor expectations.
- **EMERGING** — the change is being noticed, but the economic connection is still narrow, qualitative or lightly reflected.
- **PARTIAL** — the consequence is discussed and some estimates/valuation/narrative have adjusted, but material uncertainty or unrecognized second-order effects remain.
- **BROAD** — the specific economic consequence is widely discussed, quantified or reflected in expectations; any remaining edge must be shown rather than assumed.
- **UNRESOLVED** — evidence is insufficient to characterize recognition honestly.

Recognition state is descriptive evidence, not a numeric score and not a standalone trade signal.

## Required assessment

1. **The change and economic bridge.** State what changed and why it could matter. Keep the causal chain to roughly three to five links: observation -> behaviour/capability -> economic consequence -> company capture -> investor recognition. Separate what is verified from what is inferred and what must still happen.

2. **Market recognition / expectations gap.** This is a primary underwriting section, not a footnote. Ask:
   - What do current management guidance, consensus estimates, analyst commentary, valuation assumptions, investor discussion and the stock's relevant reaction appear to assume?
   - Is the *specific economic consequence* being discussed, or only the headline event/product?
   - What evidence suggests recognition is LOW / EMERGING / PARTIAL / BROAD / UNRESOLVED?
   - What exact connection do we believe investors may still be missing?
   - What evidence would show that our supposed edge is actually consensus?
   
   Absence of analyst or media discussion is weak evidence by itself; use a bounded, dated expectations search and disclose coverage. A stock move is evidence of repricing, not proof of what caused it.

3. **Materiality and value capture.** Test whether the change is large enough to matter to the company/security if the thesis works. Use the simplest defensible scale check: users, units, spend, capacity, market share, margin, recurring revenue, capex, supplier economics or another relevant bridge. Identify who actually captures the value and the strongest competing beneficiary or offset. Do not invent supplier share, exclusivity, take rate, ownership or conversion.

4. **Recognition path and clock.** Identify what could force broader investor recognition: a KPI, app/usage persistence, disclosed revenue, contract, backlog, guidance change, estimate revision, earnings call, launch, regulatory filing, customer disclosure, municipal/permit milestone or other observable evidence. State:
   - likely recognition window;
   - next dated checkpoint;
   - what would accelerate recognition;
   - what would delay it;
   - when the information edge expires even if the long-term company thesis remains plausible.

5. **Failure, delay/no recognition and successful recognition.** Explain each outcome, the evidence that would support it, indicative security payoff when defensible and its timing. Add a breakout case only if genuinely distinct. Use probability ranges or break-even odds only when they improve the decision and can be defended. Unknown payoff inputs remain unknown.

6. **Remaining recognition payoff at the current price.** Price matters here as **remaining payoff**, not as a mandatory Core cheapness test. Compare:
   - current price;
   - failure/delay downside;
   - a plausible **recognition scenario** if the market begins incorporating the thesis;
   - any further upside that requires actual fundamental delivery beyond recognition.

   A `recognition value` is a scenario tool, not intrinsic fair value. Label its basis: estimate revision, multiple change, sum-of-parts recognition, comparable reaction, expected earnings contribution, or another explicit method. Do not mechanically add speculative value to a Core fair value or double-count economics already embedded in consensus.

7. **Instrument and clock.** Record exact security/listing/currency, current quote and timestamp, intended horizon, recognition window, review date and falsification/loss policy. For options assess strike, expiry, premium at risk, liquidity, volatility, breakeven and whether expiry spans the *recognition* event. An option can intentionally expire before a later product launch when the thesis expects earlier recognition. A correct company thesis cannot rescue a mismatched or overpriced instrument.

8. **Challenge and allocation.** Independently challenge:
   - whether the change is actually material;
   - the weakest causal connection;
   - the claimed expectations gap;
   - evidence that recognition is already broader than assumed;
   - the failure/delay path;
   - the recognition clock and chosen instrument.

   Mark UNCHALLENGED when that pass is unavailable. Send failure/delay losses, event-gap risk, recognition horizon, exact exposure and unresolved inputs to Portfolio Capital Allocation. Do not invent the user's speculative loss budget, portfolio weight or leverage permission.

For a genuinely short-duration discrete event, obtain Event-Trade Underwriting's execution/payoff expertise as needed while preserving CAMILLO strategy identity. Do not automatically reroute every Camillo case based on a calendar threshold alone.

## Four separate decision outputs

**Change significance:** MATERIAL / POTENTIALLY MATERIAL / IMMATERIAL / UNRESOLVED, with the economic bridge and scale reason.

**Information edge:** SPECULATIVE CANDIDATE / WATCH / NO DISTINCTIVE EDGE / INVALIDATED. Always include the recognition state (LOW / EMERGING / PARTIAL / BROAD / UNRESOLVED), the specific believed expectations gap and confidence in that gap.

**Security/instrument:** actionable for speculative allocation review / unsuitable expression / insufficient remaining recognition payoff / unresolved, with the actual price, downside and recognition-window basis.

**Portfolio permission:** pending allocation / within verified approved limits / blocked or unavailable. Only a completed allocation review can support the latter determinations. A positive information-edge judgment is not a BUY instruction.

These labels are user-facing explanations, not new backend enums. When persistence requires an existing posture, use a compatible allowed posture with explicit CAMILLO-mode rationale only if its requirements are honestly satisfied. Do not coerce a Camillo result into a Core valuation slot, overwrite a Core baseline, or fabricate mandatory DCF/target fields. Preserve it as a separate dated case when storage cannot represent the distinction.

## Recognition monitoring and exit logic

The Camillo case should be re-reviewed when evidence suggests recognition is catching up, even if the company's fundamentals improve. Examples include:

- management explicitly quantifies the previously hidden opportunity;
- consensus estimates or sell-side models materially incorporate it;
- the specific second-order connection becomes common investor commentary;
- the relevant KPI/usage/revenue evidence becomes broadly reported;
- the security reprices enough that the original recognition payoff is substantially consumed;
- the expected recognition window passes without confirmation;
- the observation or causal bridge weakens.

A move from LOW -> EMERGING -> PARTIAL -> BROAD is not automatically a sale sequence. Reassess **remaining payoff** at each stage. A Camillo thesis can become EDGE EXHAUSTED while a separate Core thesis remains attractive.

Do not silently extend the horizon, average down, roll an option or convert the position to Core. Preserve the original bet and record any deliberate renewed decision.

## Output and monitor handoff

Begin:

**Strategy / Case / Stage / Horizon**

Then show, near the top:

- **What changed**
- **Why it is economically meaningful**
- **Market recognition state**
- **What we think is still missing**
- **What would force recognition**
- **Remaining recognition payoff**
- **What breaks the thesis**

Then provide the four decision outputs and one next action. Include known/inferred/bet separation, materiality/capture, expectations evidence, failure/delay/recognition scenarios, strongest challenge, review/expiry dates, exact instrument and allocation constraints. No compulsory Core-style valuation chapter.

Handoff: case revision; change significance; edge state; recognition state; specific expectations-gap hypothesis; evidence supporting/contradicting it; next recognition checkpoint; falsifier; evidence-expiry/review deadline; intended hold-through or reduce-before policy if relevant; instrument expiry; required evidence for adding; remaining recognition payoff; accepted price/risk conditions if any; and aggregate issuer/cluster exposure where verified.

Recognized information, a fading edge, missed timing or inadequate remaining payoff triggers review of the Camillo case even while the company's long-term thesis remains intact.
