# Valuation Methods and Capital Structure

Use the method that best reflects the economics of the business. Do not select a method because it produces the preferred conclusion.

## Capitalization first

Before final valuation, establish when material:

- current share price and as-of date
- basic shares
- in-the-money and material out-of-the-money options/warrants
- restricted stock / RSUs
- convertibles and preferred securities
- cash and investments
- debt and lease liabilities
- non-controlling interests
- material royalties, streams, earnouts, or other claims
- expected financing required to reach the Base case

Calculate both basic and fully diluted equity value. For capital-dependent businesses, also estimate post-financing fully diluted value per share.

## Financing gate

If meaningful outside capital is required before the thesis can succeed, estimate:

1. remaining capital requirement
2. debt capacity and likely debt cost
3. equity requirement
4. plausible equity issuance price or discount
5. incremental shares / warrants / converts
6. royalties, streams, or other financing claims
7. resulting post-financing ownership
8. resulting value per share

Do not compare an undiluted market capitalization directly with a project NPV or future enterprise value when material financing has not been incorporated.

## Operating / mature companies

Primary methods may include:

- DCF
- normalized earnings / P-E
- EV/EBITDA
- FCF yield

Useful cross-checks:

- historical trading range
- peer multiples
- normalized margins and ROIC
- replacement cost where relevant

For DCF work, make revenue, margins, reinvestment, tax, WACC, terminal growth, and terminal multiple assumptions visible. Avoid hiding a large portion of upside in terminal value.

## High-growth / platform / software companies

Primary methods may include:

- multi-year operating model plus normalized multiple
- DCF
- SOTP when business lines have different economics

Cross-check with appropriate metrics such as:

- EV/revenue
- EV/gross profit
- FCF yield on normalized economics
- growth-adjusted multiples
- cohort or unit economics

Do not use revenue multiples without discussing the margin and capital intensity required to justify them.

## Conglomerates

Prefer SOTP when materially different businesses deserve different economics or multiples.

For each segment identify:

- earnings / cash-flow basis
- valuation method
- peer or intrinsic anchor
- corporate costs
- net cash / debt
- tax leakage or holding-company discounts when material

Use consolidated DCF or market multiples as a cross-check.

## Mining and resource developers

Primary:

- project NAV / NPV

Cross-checks:

- P/NAV
- EV/resource ounce or equivalent unit
- producer/developer peer valuations
- relevant transactions

Adjust for:

- resource confidence and conversion
- recoveries
- mine plan
- operating costs
- initial and sustaining capex
- permitting
- construction timing
- financing and dilution
- royalties/streams
- taxes
- commodity price
- time to cash flow

Do not treat all resource ounces as mineable or value inferred resources identically to reserves.

## Biotech / clinical-stage healthcare

Primary:

- probability-adjusted / risk-adjusted NPV by program

Cross-checks:

- market-implied probability of success
- licensing / partnership economics
- comparable transactions
- pipeline SOTP

Explicitly model:

- development stage
- probability of technical and regulatory success
- time to approval
- addressable treated population
- pricing / net price
- penetration
- launch costs
- royalties / milestone obligations
- future R&D and financing
- patent / exclusivity duration

Do not treat probability-of-success estimates as facts.

## Banks and insurers

Use sector-appropriate methods such as:

- P/TBV
- P/B
- P/E on normalized earnings
- excess return / residual income
- dividend discount when appropriate

Focus on capital adequacy, credit quality, reserve adequacy, funding, ROE/ROTCE, and the sustainability of returns.

## REITs / real estate

Common methods:

- NAV
- P/AFFO
- cap-rate sensitivity

Reconcile debt, maturities, interest cost, occupancy, lease rollover, development commitments, and asset-level values.

## Reverse valuation — what is priced in?

Every full underwriting should attempt to infer what today's price requires.

Possible approaches:

- reverse DCF: solve for growth, margins, ROIC, or terminal economics
- reverse P/E: solve for normalized EPS required at a reasonable multiple
- mining: infer P/NAV, resource size, or commodity-price assumptions embedded in valuation
- biotech: infer probability of success or peak-sales value implied by enterprise value
- event-driven: infer completion/success probability
- marketplace/platform: infer users, take rate, volume, margin, or market share

State the result in plain language:

> At today's price, the market appears to discount approximately ______.

Then identify the measurable difference between that implied path and the underwriting Base case.

## Multiple-expansion discipline

Decompose expected upside into:

- fundamental earnings / FCF growth
- estimate revisions
- mix shift
- capital return
- net cash / debt change
- probability change
- multiple expansion or contraction

If most of the return requires multiple expansion without a clear reason the market should re-rate the security, lower conviction.

## Valuation cross-check rule

Whenever practical, use one independent cross-check that does not simply repeat the assumptions of the primary method.

Examples:

- DCF + peer multiple
- project NAV + EV/resource unit
- rNPV + market-implied probability
- SOTP + consolidated multiple

If the methods disagree materially, explain why instead of averaging them mechanically.
