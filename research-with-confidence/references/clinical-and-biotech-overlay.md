# Clinical and Biotech Research Overlay

Use this overlay for trial results, approvals, clinical holds, safety signals, medical publications, reimbursement, licensing, royalties, or biotech securities.

## Four separate layers

Do not collapse these into one conclusion.

### 1. Clinical validity

Assess:

- phase, design, randomization, blinding, control, stratification;
- population, line of therapy, biomarker, inclusion/exclusion criteria;
- primary and key secondary endpoints;
- effect size, confidence interval, p-value where relevant, and clinical significance;
- event maturity, follow-up, censoring, missing data, multiplicity, subgroup consistency;
- adverse events, severe events, deaths, discontinuations, dose reductions, and exposure time;
- protocol or analysis changes and whether they were prespecified.

A statistically significant result can still be clinically weak, immature, or difficult to generalize.

### 2. Regulatory significance

Assess:

- accepted endpoint and precedent for the indication;
- accelerated versus full approval pathway;
- filing status, review type, advisory committee, confirmatory requirements;
- label population, line, biomarker, regimen, contraindications, and safety language;
- regulator versus sponsor interpretation;
- manufacturing, inspection, or post-marketing requirements.

### 3. Commercial significance

Assess:

- addressable diagnosed and treated population;
- current standard of care and comparator strength;
- efficacy/safety/convenience differentiation;
- physician and patient switching friction;
- payer, reimbursement, testing, manufacturing, and launch readiness;
- competitive readouts and likely timing;
- partner quality, geography, commercialization rights, royalties, and milestones.

### 4. Security significance

Assess:

- enterprise value and pre-event run-up;
- cash runway, burn, financing need, dilution, and debt or royalty claims;
- actual ownership of economics by territory and indication;
- milestone probability and avoidance of double-counting with royalties or sales;
- market-implied program value or success probability;
- whether the event changes intrinsic value, probability, timing, or confidence only.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer:

1. regulator documents and approved label;
2. trial registry, protocol, statistical plan, and version history;
3. peer-reviewed paper or full conference presentation;
4. sponsor filing and release;
5. independent clinicians, statisticians, and high-quality reporting.

A sponsor top-line release without the full dataset normally supports only a preliminary conclusion.

## Pre-event expectations packet

For scheduled readouts, PDUFAs, advisory committees, or approvals, preserve before the event:

```text
known_date
current_price_and_run_up
consensus_or_market_implied_expectation
success_delay_failure_scenarios
expected_endpoint_or_label
milestone_and_royalty_economics
competitive_benchmark
what_would_constitute_a_real_surprise
```

After the event, compare the result with this packet rather than reconstructing expectations with hindsight.

## RWC output

State confidence separately for:

- validity of the clinical result;
- regulatory probability or label breadth;
- commercial adoption;
- company economic capture;
- current security mispricing.

Advance to Full Underwriting only when the clinical/regulatory thesis survives and the remaining question is security value, rNPV/SOTP, capital structure, dilution, scenario probability, or time-to-resolution.
