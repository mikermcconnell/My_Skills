## Appendix D / Section A scope summary

The City is procuring a consultant to help prepare a five-year Barrie Transit Strategic Plan for 2027 to 2032.

The plan covers:
- Conventional transit.
- Specialized transit.
- Service delivery and network structure.
- Community connections.
- Fiscal and resource needs.
- Implementation actions and decision points.

Major work areas:
- Assess peer agency services and practices.
- Engage staff, riders, community organizations, and regional partners.
- Develop strategic directions for service, infrastructure, technology, and financial planning.
- Prepare a phased year-by-year implementation roadmap.
- Establish performance measures and targets.

Project delivery model:
- Staff-led chapters: City provides major content; consultant supports formatting and integration.
- Joint chapters: City and consultant both develop content.
- Consultant-led chapters: consultant leads analysis and drafting.
- Consultant drafts and integrates Consultant-led and Joint chapter content.
- Consultant supports formatting for Staff-led chapters so the final report is consistent.

Timeline:
- Work expected to take approximately one year.
- Updated by Addendum 1: draft Strategic Plan in July 2027 and Council presentation in August 2027.

Engagement scope includes:
- MVT and terminal staff workshop.
- City departments workshop.
- Seniors & Accessibility Advisory Committee.
- Georgian College.
- RVH.
- Metrolinx / GO Transit.
- Simcoe County LINX / Ontario Northland.
- Major employer outreach.
- Virtual Public Open House.
- Online survey and BuildingBarrie.ca content.
- In-person Public Open House as a provisional item.
- Interim Council workshop.
- Final Council presentation.
- Monthly Engagement Digests.
- Engagement Summary Report.

Strategic Plan development areas include:
- Existing service profile, ridership, performance, travel patterns, regional connections, fleet, facilities, finances, and customer experience.
- Growth, land use, policy, and funding research.
- Vision, values, ridership targets, service targets, and regional connectivity targets.
- Network evolution, service standards, transit priority, infrastructure, and yearly service changes.
- Fare structure, fare technology, fare programs, and partnerships.
- Marketing and customer communications.
- Fleet, facilities, technology, emerging technologies, and AI.
- Alignment with the Official Plan, Transportation Master Plan, and BRT planning.
- Operating budget, capital budget, revenue strategy, and financial sustainability.
- Implementation plan, KPIs, targets, and go/no-go decision points.

## Section F worksheet rows

### F.1 Company Overview
Weight in RFP: 5 points. Worksheet score is 1-10.

Look for:
- Company overview.
- Years in business.
- Number of professional / technical employees.
- Primary services offered.
- Transit planning capability.
- Office locations and team resources by location.
- Subconsultants, their role, legal relationship, expertise, services, and percentage involvement.

Minimum:
- At least 5 years providing related services.
- Appropriate capability, resources, and public sector transit or transportation planning experience.

Preference:
- More than 5 years providing related services.
- Added areas of expertise or depth of experience that benefit the project.

### F.2a Project Team Overview
Weight in RFP: 4 points. Worksheet score is 1-10.

Look for:
- Organization chart.
- Named team members.
- Assigned roles.
- Reporting structure.
- Team structure and how skills map to the deliverables.
- Previous experience working together.

Minimum:
- Clear team structure for the deliverables.
- Clear Project Manager and Senior Transit Planner roles.

Preference:
- Proposed team has worked together on similar transit or transportation planning projects.
- Depth of transit-specific experience.

### F.2b Project Manager
Weight in RFP: 10 points. Worksheet score is 1-10.

Look for:
- Project Manager qualifications and CV.
- Education, credentials, and relevant experience.
- Two similar project examples from the past 5 years.
- For each example: project name, client, location, dates, value, role, and responsibilities.
- Availability for the full project.

Minimum:
- At least 5 years of relevant project management or leading-role experience.
- Experience managing multi-disciplinary teams.
- Experience with stakeholder engagement.
- Experience presenting to elected officials or senior decision-makers.
- Experience with quality control, schedule, and budget delivery.
- Availability for the full project.

Preference:
- More than 5 years of relevant experience.
- Leading role on a public sector project.
- Council approval experience for a transit strategic plan or similar transportation strategy.
- Steering committee or advisory group workshop experience.
- Experience with municipal transit agencies using a contracted operations model.
- PMP, RPP, P.Eng., or other professional qualifications that benefit the City.

### F.2c Senior Transit Planner
Weight in RFP: 10 points. Worksheet score is 1-10.

Look for:
- Senior Transit Planner qualifications and CV.
- Education, credentials, and relevant experience.
- Two similar project examples from the past 5 years.
- For each example: project name, client, location, dates, value, role, and responsibilities.

Minimum:
- At least 5 years of relevant transit or transportation planning experience.
- Experience with transit service planning, route design, frequency analysis, or network restructuring.
- Experience with peer benchmarking or best practices reviews across multiple comparable transit agencies.

Preference:
- More than 5 years of similar experience.
- Direct experience with transit strategic plans for public sector agencies.
- Ontario municipal transit experience is stronger.
- Fare analysis, fare capping, or fare technology assessment experience.
- RPP, P.Eng., or other professional qualifications that benefit the City.

### F.2d Additional Team Members
Weight in RFP: 6 points. Worksheet score is 1-10.

Look for:
- CVs for additional team members and subconsultants.
- Clear roles and responsibilities.
- Technical skills tied to the project scope.
- Work on similar projects.

Minimum:
- Team covers the full scope, including financial analysis, fare modelling, capital planning, and report production.
- Each team member has relevant qualifications, technical skills, and experience.
- Roles are clearly defined.

Preference:
- Experience with transit fare and revenue analysis.
- Transit technology systems experience, such as fare systems, real-time information, or scheduling software.
- Graphic design or report production strength.
- Public sector experience, especially municipal transit or transportation projects.

### F.4 Project Understanding and Approach
Weight in RFP: 10 points. Worksheet score is 1-10.

Look for:
- Understanding of objectives and scope.
- Understanding of Staff-led, Joint, and Consultant-led chapter model.
- Methodology for major work streams:
  - Peer benchmarking and policy research.
  - Stakeholder and public engagement.
  - Service planning and network development.
  - Fare strategy and financial analysis.
  - Capital and technology planning.
  - Plan integration and report production.
- Collaboration model with City staff.
- How the consultant will handle Staff-led inputs and Joint work.
- Project-specific risks and mitigation.
- QA/QC approach.

Minimum:
- Clear understanding of scope and chapter delivery model.
- Defined approach for achieving the deliverables.
- Clear method for stakeholder engagement and public consultation.

Preference:
- Practical approach to shared chapter delivery.
- Good understanding of project risks and realistic mitigation.
- Value-added approaches at no additional cost.
- Efficient engagement approaches that reach non-traditional transit users.

### F.5 Work Plan and Schedule
Weight in RFP: 5 points. Worksheet score is 1-10.

Look for:
- Work plan.
- Gantt chart or similar schedule.
- Tasks, milestones, critical path, meeting dates, key events, timelines, dependencies, and deliverable submissions.
- City review periods for drafts or chapters.
- Uncosted Time Task Matrix.
- Team member hours by task for Strategy Plan Development and Engagement Plan.
- No hourly rates, costs, or pricing in the uncosted matrix.

Minimum:
- Work plan addresses all chapters.
- Captures key milestones, meetings, sequencing, and City review periods.
- Shows the project can be delivered within the intended 12-month timeline.
- Reasonable allocation of effort by team member and task.
- No more than 15% of total estimated hours assigned to unnamed resources.

Preference:
- Logical sequencing with clear dependencies.
- Realistic timing for City-supplied information and City review.
- Clear and legible Gantt chart and Uncosted Time Task Matrix.
- Shows commitment to quality and consistency.
